 Analysis: In highly imbalanced text datasets (~87% ham), increasing K expands the
 neighborhood to include predominantly majority-class (ham) messages. While precision
 often stays high, spam recall declines for higher K because minority spam points are
 easily outvoted by surrounding legitimate vectors in sparse high-dimensional space.

6. PRACTICAL TRADE-OFFS & ALGORITHMIC BEHAVIOR IN TEXT CLASSIFICATION
 a. High Accuracy vs. Spam Recall Paradox:
    Because 87% of the corpus is legitimate (ham), a naive dummy classifier predicting
    'ham' for every single message would achieve ~87% accuracy while completely failing
    at spam detection (0% recall, 0% F1). Thus, Spam F1-Score and Spam Recall are the
    primary evaluation metrics for this task.

 b. False Positives (Ham classified as Spam) vs False Negatives (Spam classified as Ham):
    - False Positive Cost: An urgent legitimate SMS (e.g. bank OTP, flight alert, or family
      emergency) is blocked or diverted. This damages user trust severely.
    - False Negative Cost: An unsolicited advertisement, phishing link, or scam reaches the
      inbox, exposing the recipient to fraud.
    In production systems, a high-precision threshold is prioritized to guarantee minimal
    false alarms while maintaining satisfactory recall.

 c. High-Dimensional TF-IDF Representation:
    - Support Vector Machine (LinearSVC): Excels in text classification because high-dimensional
      sparse text representations are frequently linearly separable. L2 regularization
      and margin maximization prevent overfitting.
    - Multinomial Naive Bayes: Exceptionally fast, low-parameter, and robust with word count
      frequencies. It typically achieves outstanding precision with near-zero false alarms.
    - Decision Trees: Suffer from greedy, axis-aligned splits over thousands of sparse
      dimensions, often leading to sub-optimal generalization compared to margin classifiers.
    - KNN: Computationally expensive at inference time (O(N_train * D) per query), and
      distances in sparse TF-IDF space suffer from dimensionality degradation.

7. EVIDENCE-BASED FINAL RECOMMENDATION
 Based on the measured experimental evidence on the held-out test partition,
 Support Vector Machine is the recommended classifier for production SMS spam detection.
 It achieved the optimal balance of Spam Recall (0.9219) and
 Spam Precision (0.9147), yielding the highest F1-Score
 (0.9183) and robust generalization under class imbalance.
